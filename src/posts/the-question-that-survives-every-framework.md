Take stock of what changed in sustainability reporting over the last eighteen months.

The Omnibus package entered into force on 18 March 2026, raising CSRD scope thresholds to more than 1,000 employees and more than 450 million euro turnover, with first application for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027. The revised ESRS, adopted on 3 July 2026, cut mandatory datapoints by more than 60%. The GHG Protocol published the first substantial rework of its Scope 3 Standard since 2011. ESG rating providers came under ESMA supervision on 2 July 2026. In California, CARB moved to defer the first SB 253 reporting deadline by three months.

Read as a list, that looks like instability. Read for direction, it is remarkably consistent.

## What actually moved, and what did not

Almost every change was to **scope**, **volume**, or **timing**. Who has to report, how much they have to report, and when it is due. Those are the negotiable parts of a reporting regime, and they were negotiated hard.

What did not move is the **evidentiary standard**.

CSRD retained limited assurance from the first year of application, with EU-level standards due by July 2027. The Scope 3 revision is consulting on tiering emissions by data type and on a coverage floor with justified exclusions, both of which ask for more evidence about data quality rather than less. The ESG ratings regulation is built around transparency of methodology. Every one of those points the same way.

> Regulators reduced what they ask for. They did not reduce how well they expect you to know it.

## Why this is the durable requirement

I think there is a structural reason the provenance question keeps surviving revisions, and it is not that regulators are attached to it.

It is that sustainability data became load-bearing. Once emissions figures feed financing terms, procurement qualification, index construction, and remuneration outcomes, a wrong number stops being a reputational matter and becomes a financial one. Nobody prices a loan off an unverifiable figure knowingly.

That shift happened independently of any particular framework, and it is not reversible by simplification. You can reduce the number of datapoints a company must publish. You cannot reduce the interest that a lender has in whether the remaining ones are right.

This is why I am sceptical of the reading that the last eighteen months represent a retreat. Scope narrowed considerably. The consequences attached to what remains in scope did not narrow at all, and in some respects they intensified, since a shorter disclosure concentrates attention on every figure that survived the cut.

## The through line in this work

Looking back at what I have written over the past year, the same argument keeps appearing in different clothes.

That [ESG reporting struggled because organisations could not show their work](/blog/from-guesswork-to-audit-trails/), not because the arithmetic was hard. That [ESG controlling lives and dies on data quality](/blog/esg-data-quality/) rather than on calculation. That [AI belongs in this process as an assistant rather than an author](/blog/ai-in-esg-reporting-assistant-not-author/), because someone has to be able to account for the output. That [the EU AI Act and ESG converge on governance](/blog/eu-ai-act-esg-sustainability/), which is to say on the ability to explain how a number or a decision was reached.

None of those arguments depend on the current version of any framework. They would have been true under the first-generation ESRS and they will be true under whatever follows the revised ones.

## What I would tell someone starting now

Learn the current rules, because you need them to do the job this year. Hold them lightly, because a meaningful proportion will be different within three years.

Then spend your real effort on the part that does not get revised. Knowing where a figure came from. Being able to reproduce it. Recording the assumption with a name and a date next to it. Building the check that catches the problem before someone external does.

Every framework in this field is a particular answer to a question that predates all of them and will outlast all of them. Thresholds change, datapoints get cut, deadlines slip, and new supervisors appear over parts of the system that were previously unwatched.

The question stays exactly where it was: how do you know?
