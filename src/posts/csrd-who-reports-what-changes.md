The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive, usually shortened to **CSRD**, is the EU law that requires certain companies to include sustainability information in their management report. It is not the reporting template itself. That job belongs to the **European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)**.

That distinction is the easiest way to understand the system: the CSRD establishes *who must report and the legal obligations around the report*; ESRS establish *what the report must contain and how the information is prepared*.

The rules have changed materially since the CSRD was adopted in 2022. The Omnibus I amending Directive entered into force on 18 March 2026 and narrowed the future scope. As at 1 September 2026, companies therefore need to check the amended legislation and its national transposition rather than rely on an old CSRD scope chart.

## The essential terminology

**Directive.** An EU directive sets results that Member States must achieve through national law. This is different from an EU regulation, which is directly applicable. The CSRD amended the EU Accounting Directive and must be reflected in each Member State’s legislation.

**Management report.** The narrative report accompanying a company’s annual financial statements. CSRD sustainability information appears in a dedicated section of this report rather than in an unrelated voluntary publication.

**Sustainability statement.** The dedicated section prepared under ESRS. It covers material environmental, social and governance impacts, risks and opportunities.

**Double materiality.** A sustainability matter is reportable when it is material from the impact perspective, the financial perspective, or both. *Impact materiality* concerns significant effects on people or the environment. *Financial materiality* concerns sustainability matters that could affect the company’s financial position, performance, cash flows, access to finance or cost of capital.

**Value chain.** Activities, resources and relationships connected with the company’s business model, upstream and downstream. It includes more than the company’s own legal entities and is not limited to direct suppliers.

**Limited assurance.** An independent practitioner performs procedures and obtains enough evidence to express a limited level of confidence over the reported sustainability information. It is less extensive than reasonable assurance, but it is not a light editorial review.

## Who is in scope after Omnibus I?

For financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027, the amended Accounting Directive generally brings an EU undertaking into the individual reporting scope when it exceeds both:

- €450 million in net turnover; and
- an average of 1,000 employees during the financial year.

Equivalent tests apply at consolidated group level for parent undertakings. Separate provisions cover issuers and non-EU groups, so a group with cross-border operations should not make its scope decision from the two headline thresholds alone.

The chronology matters. The first-wave companies reported under the original timetable for financial year 2024, in reports published in 2025. The 2026 amendments then introduced transitional relief, including the possibility for Member States to exempt first-wave undertakings that fall below the new thresholds from reporting for financial years beginning in 2025 and 2026. National implementation must therefore be checked.

> “Not in the future scope” and “no information will be requested” are not the same conclusion.

Smaller companies can still receive sustainability questions from customers, banks and investors. The amended rules introduce a **value-chain cap** intended to limit what reporting companies may demand from protected undertakings with up to 1,000 employees. A separate voluntary reporting standard is designed to provide a common ceiling and vocabulary for those requests.

## What an in-scope company has to do

The core obligation is to disclose material sustainability information in accordance with ESRS. In practice, that requires a repeatable reporting system rather than a one-off writing exercise.

The company needs to:

1. establish its reporting perimeter and value-chain approach;
2. identify and assess material impacts, risks and opportunities;
3. prepare the required governance, strategy, policy, action, metric and target disclosures;
4. connect sustainability information with the financial statements and management report;
5. retain evidence for calculations, judgements and exclusions;
6. obtain the assurance required under the applicable national law; and
7. prepare the information for the prescribed digital-reporting format when those tagging requirements apply.

An **impact, risk or opportunity**, often abbreviated to **IRO**, is the basic unit of the ESRS assessment. An impact describes an effect on people or the environment. A risk or opportunity describes a sustainability-related matter that may affect the company financially. One event can be both: water stress can harm a local ecosystem and also interrupt production.

## What the simplification did—and did not—change

The 2026 amendments substantially reduced the population expected to report. The Commission also adopted revised ESRS on 3 July 2026, with fewer datapoints and additional reliefs. On the verification date of this article, that delegated act was not yet in force; the Commission’s official register still described it as awaiting publication in the Official Journal. Its text provides for mandatory application from financial year 2027 and an option to apply it for financial year 2026 once legally effective.

Simplification did not remove double materiality, value-chain considerations, public reporting or assurance. It concentrated those obligations on a smaller group of companies and sought to limit information demands passed down to smaller business partners.

For reporting teams, the practical lesson is to separate four questions that are often mixed together:

- Is the legal entity or group in scope?
- Which reporting year and national transposition apply?
- Which version of ESRS applies or may be elected?
- Which information is material for this particular company?

Only after answering all four does a datapoint-level gap assessment become reliable.
